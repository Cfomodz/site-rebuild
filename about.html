About

Damon P. Anderson

A transformational change agent with more than 25 years of executive and on-the-ground experience across the public, military, and private sectors. Based in Lancaster, Pennsylvania.

“I retired, led a lot, built some firsts, lifted leaders. I'm just getting started.”

Damon started as a programmer analyst in the U.S. Army and went on to give 29½ years to the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania. At the Department of Community Affairs he partnered with IBM to install the agency's first network and computer systems. At the Department of Conservation and Natural Resources he was appointed the first Chief Information Officer, built the IT organization from the ground up, and launched Pennsylvania's first Parks Reservation and Revenue System.

He retired early in 2010 to found DP Anderson Consulting and to build The Strategic Alliance Experts with one of his mentors, then returned to state service in 2014 as CIO of the Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission, where he also served as acting Chief Information Security Officer. There he built the information security team, set up Commission-wide IT governance, and started an AI and emerging technologies practice.

He retired from public service in January 2026. What he says he is proudest of is the leaders he developed, many of whom now serve as CIOs and executives across the Commonwealth.

Now he does business development and works as a solutions architect and problem solver for small and mid-sized businesses, nonprofits, houses of worship, and community organizations. He serves on the transition team at J. Cooper Jr. LLC, leading workforce development on the Rockton Hospital Repurposing Project. He is pursuing a Ph.D. in cybersecurity leadership and preparing security products for ordinary people. He is also a minister, presenter, advocate, counselor, and musician.

How he works

Three principles

Automate everything

Repeating work belongs to systems. That frees people for the decisions only people can make.

Leave it better

Every engagement should leave the client more capable, and every project should return something to its community.

Protect the people nobody is protecting

Security has to work for a retiree with a phone as well as it works for a company with a budget.
